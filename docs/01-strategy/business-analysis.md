# Business Analysis

## 1. Business model

The public-facing material positions Green Grid as a solar equipment and installation solutions provider. The brochure advertises solar systems and brands across panels, batteries and inverters. The exact legal entity, business structure, supplier relationships and current service coverage remain to be confirmed.

### Proposed revenue streams to validate

1. Residential rooftop solar system supply and installation.
2. Commercial / industrial solar projects.
3. Solar panels, inverters, batteries and accessories.
4. Battery or inverter replacement.
5. Inspection, maintenance and cleaning services.
6. Repair and after-sales service.
7. Extended service arrangements, if offered.

Do not assume every revenue stream is currently offered. Management must confirm each one.

## 2. Customer value proposition

The platform should help customers:
- Understand the difference between on-grid, off-grid and hybrid systems.
- Understand what system size may suit their usage and property.
- Compare equipment based on verified specifications.
- Understand costs, exclusions, warranty and subsidy eligibility.
- Request a site-specific technical assessment.
- Receive a clear itemised quotation.
- Follow project progress and contact support.

## 3. Operating lifecycle

1. Visitor discovers Green Grid.
2. Visitor reads solution and product information.
3. Visitor uses an estimator or submits an enquiry.
4. Sales staff reviews and qualifies the lead.
5. Staff contacts customer and schedules a survey.
6. Surveyor inspects roof, electrical setup, shading, access and load requirements.
7. Technical team prepares a design and bill of materials.
8. Sales staff issues an itemised quotation.
9. Customer asks questions, requests revisions or accepts.
10. Agreement and payment terms are recorded.
11. Materials are procured and allocated.
12. Installation is scheduled and completed.
13. Testing, commissioning and applicable utility / scheme steps are documented.
14. Customer receives handover documents and warranty information.
15. Support, maintenance and warranty requests are managed over time.

## 5. Business rules requiring confirmation

- Serviceable PIN codes and travel charges.
- Whether initial consultation is free.
- Whether site inspection is free or chargeable.
- Whether booking requires an advance.
- Quotation validity period.
- Payment milestones.
- Cancellation, rescheduling and refund rules.
- Who can approve discounts.
- Who signs off technical designs.
- Who is responsible for official portal / utility applications.
- Who registers products and warranties.
- Service hours and emergency escalation.
- Whether the company sells products separately from installation.

## 6. Pricing and subsidy control

The brochure and previously reviewed website calculator show different 3 kW price ranges. The business must supply dated quotations and package BOMs to establish the reason.

Every public package or calculator result must identify:
- System configuration.
- Included and excluded items.
- Tax treatment.
- Transport / civil / electrical charges.
- Battery inclusion or exclusion.
- Mounting structure assumptions.
- Subsidy estimate and eligibility caveat.
- Quote date and validity.
- Whether price is indicative or confirmed.

Subsidy must be displayed separately from the company's selling price. Eligibility and process must be checked against current official scheme guidance before publication.
